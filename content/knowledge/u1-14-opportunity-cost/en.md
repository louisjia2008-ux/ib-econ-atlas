---
title: Opportunity cost
takeaway: Opportunity cost is the value of the best feasible alternative forgone
  when a choice is made, not the sum of every rejected option.
definition: Opportunity cost is the benefit or value of the next-best feasible
  alternative that cannot be obtained once resources are committed to a chosen
  use.
causalChain:
  - Scarcity creates competing uses
  - Feasible alternatives are ranked by value
  - The highest-ranked option is selected
  - The second-ranked forgone option is the opportunity cost
realWorldExample:
  title: Alternative uses of a public budget
  sourceUrl: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/FM
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The IMF Fiscal Monitor discusses government spending and fiscal
    space. A new programme uses finance and administrative capacity; its
    opportunity cost is the best alternative policy that those resources could
    have supported.
misconceptions:
  - Opportunity cost is not the total of all forgone options; it is the best one.
  - A sunk cost that cannot be recovered is not the opportunity cost of a
    decision made now.
examApplication:
  - "Use the complete statement: the opportunity cost of choosing A is the best
    forgone alternative B."
  - On a PPC, measure the cost by the reduction in output of the other good.
summary:
  - Opportunity cost follows from scarcity and choice.
  - Only the best forgone alternative counts.
  - Cost may be expressed in money, time, output, or well-being.
---
Selecting one use prevents the same resource from being devoted fully to another. Economics calls the most valuable rejected option the opportunity cost, making the true trade-off in a decision visible.

The cost depends on the feasible alternatives and the decision-maker's objectives at that moment, so it is not a fixed cash amount. It can take the form of lost income, time, output, or environmental quality. An impossible alternative should not be counted.
