---
title: Positive economics
takeaway: Positive economics makes testable claims about facts, relationships,
  or causes and asks whether evidence supports or challenges them.
definition: Positive economics describes and explains economic reality through
  statements that can in principle be tested with observation, data, or
  experiments, without concluding what ought to be done from a value judgement.
causalChain:
  - State a claim about a fact or causal relationship
  - Translate concepts into observable measures
  - Collect and analyse relevant evidence
  - Provisionally support, revise, or reject the claim
realWorldExample:
  title: Open data permit a growth claim to be tested
  sourceUrl: https://data.worldbank.org/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The World Bank data platform publishes growth, price, trade, and
    social indicators. A claim that real GDP growth fell in a specified year can
    be checked against a defined measure and is positive.
misconceptions:
  - A positive statement can be false; testable does not mean already proven.
  - Numbers appear objective, but definitions, samples, and causal methods still
    require judgement.
examApplication:
  - Look for measurable variables and distinguish is or will from should or
    better.
  - State what evidence would be needed and which other factors could confound
    the relationship.
summary:
  - Positive claims concern observable facts or causes.
  - Evidence can support or refute them.
  - Measurement and identification affect credibility.
---
Positive analysis asks what the economy is like and how variables relate. The prediction that a higher tax will reduce consumption is testable and remains a positive proposition even if research eventually finds no reduction.

Testing is rarely automatic. Income, other policies, and expectations may change at the same time. Researchers define measures, choose comparison groups, and address alternative explanations to distinguish correlation from a credible causal account.
