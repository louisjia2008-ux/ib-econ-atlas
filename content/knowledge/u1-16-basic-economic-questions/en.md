---
title: The three basic economic questions
takeaway: Every economic system must decide what and how much to produce, how to
  produce it, and for whom; systems differ in who decides and which mechanism
  they use.
definition: The three basic economic questions ask which goods and services to
  produce and in what quantities, which resources and techniques to use, and
  which people or groups ultimately receive the output.
causalChain:
  - Scarce resources have competing uses
  - Society determines the output mix and quantities
  - Producers select inputs, technology, and organisation
  - Income and allocation rules determine access to output
realWorldExample:
  title: Agriculture faces all three questions
  sourceUrl: https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/agriculture/overview
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: World Bank agricultural work covers crop choices, production
    techniques, and food affordability. The use of finite land, the production
    method, and access to food map directly onto what, how, and for whom.
misconceptions:
  - For whom does not ask who the producer is; it asks who can or may obtain the
    output.
  - Affluent economies still face all three questions because resources and
    objectives continue to compete.
examApplication:
  - Label what/how much, how, and for whom separately in a case.
  - Compare systems through their decision-makers, information sources, and
    distribution rules.
summary:
  - The output mix answers what and how much.
  - Inputs and technology answer how.
  - Income, prices, or administrative rules answer for whom.
---
Scarcity prevents a society from producing every desired good without limit. It must first establish an output mix and then organise land, labour, capital, and entrepreneurship to create it.

Distribution remains after production. Income and prices strongly influence access in a market economy, while public services, taxation, or rationing can alter that outcome. The questions interact because production affects income and distribution shapes future demand.
