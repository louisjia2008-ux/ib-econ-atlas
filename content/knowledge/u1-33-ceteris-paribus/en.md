---
title: Ceteris paribus
takeaway: Ceteris paribus temporarily holds other relevant influences constant
  to isolate one variable; an applied prediction must ask whether those
  conditions are genuinely similar.
definition: Ceteris paribus means assuming that relevant influences other than
  the variable under study remain unchanged so that one relationship can be
  analysed separately.
causalChain:
  - An outcome is influenced by several factors
  - The analyst selects one variable of interest
  - Other influences are temporarily held constant
  - The variable's independent directional effect is derived
realWorldExample:
  title: An interest-rate analysis requires conditions
  sourceUrl: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The Bank of England explains monetary transmission through
    borrowing, spending, and expectations. If energy prices or fiscal policy
    change at the same time, the observed inflation path cannot be attributed to
    interest rates alone.
misconceptions:
  - Ceteris paribus does not claim that other factors remain unchanged in the
    real world.
  - It isolates a directional relationship without guaranteeing the size or
    timing of an actual outcome.
examApplication:
  - Name the important factors being held constant rather than merely adding the
    Latin phrase.
  - In evaluation, identify the condition most likely to change and alter the
    prediction.
summary:
  - The assumption isolates one relationship.
  - It is an analytical device, not a description of reality.
  - Relaxing it can produce effective evaluation.
---
Price, income, preferences, and expectations can influence a choice simultaneously. To study the effect of price itself, an analyst temporarily fixes income, preferences, and other influences rather than mixing several changes together.

Application returns the analysis to reality. A substantial change in a held-constant factor can reinforce, offset, or reverse the model's prediction. Ceteris paribus therefore defines both the value and the limit of the result.
