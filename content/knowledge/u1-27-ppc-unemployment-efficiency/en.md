---
title: Unemployment and efficiency on a PPC
takeaway: A point inside the PPC indicates that current capacity is underused or
  used inefficiently; a point on the line is productively efficient, and
  returning to it can raise one or both outputs.
definition: A point inside a PPC represents actual output below the feasible
  maximum because of unemployment, idle capacity, or inefficient allocation,
  while a point on the curve represents productive efficiency for the given
  resources and technology.
causalChain:
  - Some labour or capital is idle or mismatched
  - Actual output lies within the production boundary
  - Recovery, retraining, or better management raises utilisation
  - The economy moves toward the frontier and actual output grows
realWorldExample:
  title: Labour underutilisation restricts actual output
  sourceUrl: https://ilostat.ilo.org/topics/unemployment-and-labour-underutilization/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: ILOSTAT distinguishes unemployment from broader labour
    underutilisation. When available labour is not engaged in effective
    production, an economy may operate inside its existing productive capacity.
misconceptions:
  - An interior point is not caused only by unemployment; idle equipment and
    misallocation can also create inefficiency.
  - Every point on the PPC is productively efficient but not necessarily
    allocatively efficient.
examApplication:
  - Use an arrow from inside the curve to the boundary to show improved resource
    use.
  - Distinguish this rise in actual output from an outward shift representing
    greater potential.
summary:
  - Inside the curve indicates underuse or inefficiency.
  - On the curve indicates productive efficiency.
  - Moving to the frontier does not require capacity itself to expand.
---
With unemployed labour, idle machinery, or poor resource allocation, at least one output can rise without reducing the other. The actual combination therefore lies within the frontier.

Using idle factors or improving organisation moves output toward the curve. This is growth in actual production or recovery using existing capacity. Reaching the curve establishes productive efficiency, not that society has selected its preferred output mix.
