---
title: Utility theory and marginalism
takeaway: Marginalism explains choice through the benefit and cost of one more
  unit, shifting value theory from totals and production cost toward evaluation
  at the margin.
definition: Utility theory treats satisfaction from consumption as a basis for
  choice. Marginalism studies the extra utility, output, or cost created by a
  small increase and explains decisions through marginal comparison.
causalChain:
  - An agent already holds or produces some quantity
  - One more unit creates a marginal benefit or utility
  - Obtaining that unit also creates marginal cost
  - The agent adjusts until a further change no longer improves the objective
realWorldExample:
  title: Marginal thinking explains priorities for water use
  sourceUrl: https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/Marginalism.html
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The Econlib account of marginalism explains that value depends on
    the use of an additional unit. Water has enormous total value, yet when
    supply is abundant, the next unit may serve a low-priority use and carry a
    low marginal value.
misconceptions:
  - Marginal does not mean unimportant; it means the additional unit involved in
    a change.
  - High total utility does not imply high marginal utility from the final unit.
examApplication:
  - Use marginal benefit and marginal cost to explain where adjustment stops.
  - Distinguish total, average, and marginal values and identify the additional
    unit being compared.
summary:
  - Choice takes place at the margin.
  - Marginal utility commonly falls as consumption rises.
  - Optimal adjustment depends on marginal benefit relative to marginal cost.
---
Classical accounts of value placed substantial weight on production cost. Nineteenth-century marginalists argued that an actual choice depends on the use and satisfaction available from the next unit rather than the total value of all units.

Marginal analysis became a central method in modern microeconomics. Consumers compare the benefit of extra consumption with price, and firms compare extra revenue with extra cost. The change need not be literally infinitesimal; the focus is the decision edge.
