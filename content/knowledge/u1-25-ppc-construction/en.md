---
title: Constructing a PPC
takeaway: A PPC joins the maximum feasible combinations of two output categories
  for a stated period, stock of resources, and technology.
definition: A production possibility curve (PPC) is the boundary of maximum
  combinations of two goods or services that an economy can produce when
  resources and technology are given and resources are fully and efficiently
  employed.
causalChain:
  - Fix the period, resource stock, and technology
  - Aggregate output into two labelled axes
  - Find maximum output under alternative resource allocations
  - Join the efficient combinations to form the frontier
realWorldExample:
  title: Aggregating actual industry data into two outputs
  sourceUrl: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NV.IND.TOTL.ZS
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: World Bank industry value-added data reveal the structure of
    output. A classroom PPC might place industry and other output on its axes,
    provided it acknowledges the substantial simplification of a multi-product
    economy.
misconceptions:
  - The axes measure quantities of output, not prices or the amount of inputs.
  - The frontier is not permanently fixed; changes in resources or technology
    shift it.
examApplication:
  - Label both products and units and name the curve clearly.
  - Before interpreting the PPC, state assumptions about given resources,
    technology, and efficient production.
summary:
  - The PPC is the boundary of maximum feasible output combinations.
  - It reduces many outputs to two categories.
  - Its position depends on assumptions about resources, technology, and
    efficiency.
---
The PPC displays a resource constraint by assuming that an economy produces just two categories of output. Reallocating factors from one output to the other generates a set of maximum combinations; connecting them creates the curve.

The line is the boundary of the feasible set, while combinations inside it reflect unused or inefficiently used resources. The model does not claim that an economy literally produces only two products. Two axes make trade-offs and productive capacity visible.
