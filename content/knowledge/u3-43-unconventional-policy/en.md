The central bank buys financial assets.  asset prices and liquidity alter financing conditions.  spending responses depend on credit demand and confidence.

### Original scenario and reasoning

Illustrative example: central-bank purchases of long-term bonds may lower long-term yields, but more reserves do not compel banks to lend. Asset price increases may benefit asset owners disproportionately, and policy reversal can create market risks.

Reserve growth is not proportional growth in consumption, lending or CPI.

Trace rates through spending to AD, stating exchange-rate arrangements, lags and limitations.
