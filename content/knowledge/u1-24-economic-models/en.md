---
title: Purposes and limits of economic models
takeaway: An economic model deliberately simplifies reality to isolate important
  relationships; the best model is useful for a question under clear
  assumptions, not the one with the most detail.
definition: An economic model is a purposeful simplification of agents,
  constraints, and relationships, expressed verbally, graphically, or
  mathematically to explain a mechanism and generate testable predictions.
causalChain:
  - A researcher specifies a focused question
  - Variables and relationships judged essential are retained
  - Assumptions isolate a mechanism and yield predictions
  - Predictions are compared with evidence and the model is revised
realWorldExample:
  title: A central bank models policy transmission
  sourceUrl: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/education/education-resources
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: Bank of England education resources organise interest rates,
    consumption, investment, output, and inflation into simplified
    relationships. The model clarifies transmission while expectations,
    financial conditions, and lags shape the actual effect.
misconceptions:
  - An unrealistic detail does not automatically make a model useless; the test
    is whether the simplification damages the mechanism being studied.
  - A model conclusion is a conditional prediction, not a guarantee for every
    time and place.
examApplication:
  - State key assumptions before using a diagram, then relax one assumption in
    evaluation.
  - Explain both what the model illuminates and which relevant variables or
    groups it omits.
summary:
  - Models compress complexity into selected relationships.
  - Assumptions define the range of a conclusion.
  - Predictions must face evidence and revision.
---
An economy contains too many changing elements to analyse simultaneously. A model selects variables, sets assumptions, and expresses relationships so that one mechanism becomes intelligible. The PPC, supply and demand, and AD–AS are all models.

Simplification is both the source of power and a limitation. Omitting an irrelevant detail may be harmless; omitting a factor that changes the direction of a result is not. Economists confront predictions with evidence rather than treating a model as a miniature photograph of reality.
