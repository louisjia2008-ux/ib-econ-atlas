Compare the price index with base-period 100.  divide nominal value by the index and multiply by 100.  keep units and valuation consistent.

### Original scenario and reasoning

Illustrative example: nominal GDP is 660 and the deflator 110, giving real GDP of 600. The output approach sums value added. The income approach sums compensation and operating surplus, with taxes less subsidies on production/imports needed for market-price consistency. Do not mix valuation bases.

An index of 110 means prices are 10% above the base, not necessarily 10% inflation in the latest year.

State indicators, formulas, units and limits; distinguish nominal, real, aggregate and per-capita measures.
