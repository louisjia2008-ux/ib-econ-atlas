---
title: Increasing and constant opportunity cost
takeaway: Specialised resources normally create a bowed-out PPC and increasing
  opportunity cost; identical, perfectly transferable resources create a
  straight line and constant cost.
definition: Increasing opportunity cost means that successive additions to one
  output require ever larger reductions in the other, while constant opportunity
  cost means that the trade-off ratio does not change.
causalChain:
  - Resources differ in suitability for the two outputs
  - Resources best suited to the expanding good move first
  - Further expansion transfers increasingly unsuitable factors
  - Each extra unit requires a larger loss of the other output
realWorldExample:
  title: Land differs in its suitability for alternative uses
  sourceUrl: https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/RL
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: FAOSTAT land-use data distinguish arable land, pasture, and other
    uses. Soil, climate, and location differ, so moving progressively more land
    toward one crop is likely to involve rising opportunity cost.
misconceptions:
  - A bowed shape describes changing opportunity cost, not economic growth.
  - The negative slope displays a trade-off; its absolute value is used when
    comparing opportunity cost.
examApplication:
  - Explain increasing cost through heterogeneous resources rather than merely
    saying the curve is bowed.
  - Calculate opportunity cost from coordinate changes and include units.
summary:
  - Heterogeneous and specialised resources generate increasing cost.
  - Homogeneous transferable resources can generate constant cost.
  - The absolute slope of a PPC reflects opportunity cost.
---
Factors of production are rarely equally productive in every use. Expansion of one good first draws resources well suited to it; further expansion requires resources with stronger productivity in the other activity, so the forgone amount rises.

If every resource has the same productivity ratio in both uses, each transfer creates the same trade-off and the PPC is a straight line. That case is a useful benchmark, while resource specificity is more common in practice.
