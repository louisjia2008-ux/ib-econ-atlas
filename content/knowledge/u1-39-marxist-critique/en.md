---
title: The Marxist critique
takeaway: Marxist analysis centres ownership, class relations, and capital
  accumulation, questioning whether formal freedom in exchange conceals power
  and distributional conflict in production.
definition: The Marxist critique uses ownership of the means of production,
  class relations between labour and capital, surplus value, and accumulation to
  analyse distribution, power, and tendencies toward crisis in capitalism.
causalChain:
  - Ownership of productive assets is concentrated among capital owners
  - Workers sell labour power for wages
  - A surplus between output and labour compensation is accumulated
  - Accumulation changes power, technology, distribution, and potential conflict
realWorldExample:
  title: Platform work renews questions about control and surplus
  sourceUrl: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marx/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Marx outlines
    alienation, class, and historical analysis. Algorithmic control, asset
    ownership, and labour income in digital platforms can be studied through
    those questions, while modern evidence remains necessary.
misconceptions:
  - Marxist critique is not merely a demand for equal income; it begins with
    production relations and ownership.
  - A historical theory cannot substitute directly for empirical study of
    current firms, labour markets, and institutions.
examApplication:
  - Make the mechanism from ownership through labour, surplus, and accumulation
    explicit.
  - Evaluate insights about power and distribution against predictions,
    institutional diversity, and historical evidence.
summary:
  - Ownership structures influence power in production and distribution.
  - Surplus and accumulation are central analytical ideas.
  - Class analysis looks behind exchange to production relations.
---
Marx treated capitalism as a historical mode of production, not simply a collection of voluntary trades. Workers lack ownership of the main productive assets and participate by selling labour power, while capital owners control investment and the use of surplus.

The framework connects technological change, concentration, and crisis to accumulation. Application should separate analytical concepts, historical predictions, and normative positions and test which mechanisms retain explanatory value in contemporary institutions.
