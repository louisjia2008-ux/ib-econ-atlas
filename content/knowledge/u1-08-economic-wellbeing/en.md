---
title: Economic well-being
takeaway: Economic well-being concerns whether people can live secure lives with
  meaningful choices; income matters, but it is not the whole outcome.
definition: Economic well-being is the extent to which people or groups can meet
  needs and improve quality of life through income, consumption, public
  services, health, education, security, and genuine opportunities.
causalChain:
  - Resources and public services shape available opportunities
  - Income and prices determine purchasing power
  - Health, education, and security expand or restrict capabilities
  - Together these conditions influence quality of life and lived experience
realWorldExample:
  title: Human development measurement goes beyond income
  sourceUrl: https://hdr.undp.org/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: UNDP Human Development Reports combine longevity, education, and
    income. The approach recognises that rising income per person may support
    well-being without fully representing human capabilities or life quality.
misconceptions:
  - A rise in GDP or average income does not show that every group is better off.
  - Well-being has subjective aspects, but health, education, and security still
    provide observable evidence.
examApplication:
  - When evaluating growth, distinguish average income, its distribution, and
    non-income dimensions of life quality.
  - Before drawing a conclusion from an indicator, explain what it captures and
    what it omits.
summary:
  - Income is one means to well-being, not the whole of it.
  - Public services and human capabilities matter.
  - Average measures can conceal differences between groups.
---
Income enables people to obtain housing, food, and other goods, but quality of life also depends on public health care, education, safety, the environment, and control over time. Economic well-being is therefore broader than a single output measure.

Measurement should combine averages with distribution. Income per person may rise even while a lower-income group sees no improvement in purchasing power, environmental quality, or job security. Multidimensional indices add information, although the weights assigned to their components also express values.
