---
title: Microeconomics
takeaway: Microeconomics begins with consumers, workers, firms, and individual
  markets to explain prices, output, and resource allocation.
definition: Microeconomics studies the choices and interactions of individual
  economic agents and particular markets, including their effects on prices,
  quantities, efficiency, and equity.
causalChain:
  - Consumers and firms face constraints
  - They respond to prices and incentives
  - Supply and demand interact in a particular market
  - Price, quantity, and welfare outcomes emerge
realWorldExample:
  title: A competition authority investigates a market
  sourceUrl: https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/competition-and-markets-authority
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The UK Competition and Markets Authority examines firm conduct and
    consumer outcomes in particular sectors such as housing, retail, and digital
    platforms. Its focus on participants and market rules is distinctly
    microeconomic.
misconceptions:
  - Micro refers to the unit of analysis, not to the importance of the issue.
  - Studying one firm still requires attention to consumers, rivals, and the
    institutional setting.
examApplication:
  - Identify the agent or specific market first, then trace how an incentive
    changes behaviour.
  - For welfare analysis, separate consequences for consumers, producers,
    government, and third parties.
summary:
  - Microeconomics focuses on agents and particular markets.
  - Prices and incentives connect individual decisions.
  - Outcomes can be judged through both efficiency and equity.
---
Microeconomics places the analytical lens on the building blocks of an economy. It asks why consumers buy, how workers allocate time, why firms alter output, and how those decisions meet in a particular market.

A typical argument starts from constraints and incentives and follows them to equilibrium price, traded quantity, and the gains or losses of different groups. The same reasoning supports analysis of taxes, subsidies, price controls, and competition policy.
