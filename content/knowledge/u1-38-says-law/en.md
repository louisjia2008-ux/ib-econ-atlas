title: Say's Law
takeaway: Say's Law emphasises that production creates income and supports
  demand, casting doubt on a persistent general glut; Keynes later challenged
  the speed and reliability of that adjustment.
definition: "Say's Law is commonly summarised as supply creating corresponding
  demand: production generates income, giving the economy purchasing power to
  absorb output, so broad and lasting deficient demand should not persist in
  flexible markets."
causalChain:

  - Income funds consumption or saving that can finance investment
  - Price and interest-rate adjustment recoordinates aggregate supply and demand

  - Production generates income and potential purchasing power.
  - The classical account relies on price and interest-rate adjustment.

---
Say's Law emerges from the classical emphasis on the circular connection between production and income. Producing goods also pays income to workers and resource owners, so aggregate supply creates the source of purchasing power.

The dispute concerns whether that income reliably returns as expenditure. If interest-rate adjustment channels saving into investment, a general glut should be temporary. If uncertainty raises saving while investment remains weak, aggregate demand can fall below full-employment output.
