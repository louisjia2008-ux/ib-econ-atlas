---
title: Macroeconomics
takeaway: Macroeconomics examines economy-wide performance and how policy
  influences growth, employment, prices, and external relationships.
definition: Macroeconomics treats the economy as a whole and studies aggregate
  output, growth, unemployment, inflation, income distribution, and the balance
  of payments, together with the links among them.
causalChain:
  - Households, firms, and governments make many decisions
  - Those decisions aggregate into economy-wide demand and supply
  - Growth, employment, and the price level change
  - Fiscal and monetary policy seek to stabilise or redirect outcomes
realWorldExample:
  title: The World Economic Outlook compares macro performance
  sourceUrl: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The IMF World Economic Outlook brings together growth, inflation,
    and unemployment measures across economies. It reveals aggregate trends and
    policy conditions rather than the market for one product.
misconceptions:
  - Aggregate data combine individual outcomes, but one person's experience
    cannot establish the condition of the entire economy.
  - Growth, low inflation, and low unemployment can be joint objectives while
    still creating policy trade-offs.
examApplication:
  - Organise an answer around aggregate variables and make the transmission
    mechanism explicit.
  - Evaluation should consider time lags, distributional effects, and the
    difference between short-run and long-run outcomes.
summary:
  - Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole.
  - Growth, employment, and price stability are central indicators.
  - Policy results depend on transmission, timing, and context.
---
Macroeconomics combines activity across many agents and markets to examine total output, income, employment, and the general price level. It addresses why recessions occur, what drives inflation, and how living standards might rise over time.

The direction of a policy is not enough to predict its effect. A rise in government spending or a change in the policy interest rate influences the economy only through responses by households, firms, banks, and financial markets.
