---
title: Resource allocation and reallocation
takeaway: Allocation directs factors of production; changes in prices, policy,
  or preferences cause reallocation and may address over- or underallocation.
definition: Resource allocation is the assignment of productive factors to
  different uses. Reallocation changes those uses, while overallocation and
  underallocation mean that too many or too few resources are devoted to an
  activity relative to the social optimum.
causalChain:
  - Alternative uses compete for scarce factors
  - Prices, returns, and policy signal relative value
  - Resources move toward uses with higher expected returns
  - External costs or benefits can create over- or underallocation
realWorldExample:
  title: Carbon pricing encourages reallocation
  sourceUrl: https://carbonpricingdashboard.worldbank.org/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The World Bank Carbon Pricing Dashboard records carbon taxes and
    emissions trading systems. Once emissions carry a cost, high-carbon activity
    becomes less attractive and capital or innovation may shift toward
    lower-carbon uses.
misconceptions:
  - Reallocation does not create resources; it changes how the same resources
    are used.
  - A large market allocation is not necessarily socially optimal when prices
    omit external costs.
examApplication:
  - State both the use resources leave and the use they enter.
  - Explain over- or underallocation through social marginal costs and benefits,
    not output size alone.
summary:
  - Allocation determines the uses of productive factors.
  - Changing incentives cause reallocation.
  - Omitted external effects can separate market and social outcomes.
---
Resource allocation places land, labour, capital, and entrepreneurship in different productive activities. Market prices and profit, government budgets and rules, and household preferences can all direct those flows.

Over- or underallocation requires a social benchmark. An activity may attract excessive resources if third-party costs are absent from its price. Education and similar activities may receive too little market investment when some benefits accrue beyond the buyer.
