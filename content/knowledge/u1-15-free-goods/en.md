---
title: Free goods
takeaway: A free good is abundant relative to demand in a particular place and
  time, so obtaining more does not displace another output; a zero price alone
  is not enough.
definition: A free good is a good or resource so abundant relative to demand
  that an additional unit has no significant opportunity cost under the
  conditions being considered.
causalChain:
  - A resource is highly abundant in a specific context
  - Extra use does not exclude a feasible use by someone else
  - Marginal opportunity cost is approximately zero
  - No price mechanism is needed to allocate that marginal unit
realWorldExample:
  title: Clean air can cease to be a free good
  sourceUrl: https://www.who.int/health-topics/air-pollution
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The World Health Organization documents the health burden of air
    pollution. Air has no ordinary purchase price, yet when emissions reduce air
    quality, additional pollution imposes opportunity costs and clean air cannot
    simply be treated as free.
misconceptions:
  - A zero-priced service may be tax-funded or rationed and still carry an
    opportunity cost.
  - A resource may be free in one location but scarce in a crowded or polluted
    setting.
examApplication:
  - Test marginal opportunity cost rather than looking only at the observed
    price.
  - Use location, time, and environmental conditions to explain why the
    classification can change.
summary:
  - A free good has approximately zero marginal opportunity cost.
  - Zero price does not mean zero resource cost.
  - Whether a good is free or economic depends on context.
---
Economic goods require allocation because obtaining more displaces another use. A free good is sufficiently abundant in a given context that one extra unit involves no meaningful sacrifice.

The category is not a permanent property of an object. Clean air, open space, or water may move from abundance to scarcity across times and places. Education or health care provided without a user charge still uses labour, buildings, and equipment, so it is not free in the economic sense.
