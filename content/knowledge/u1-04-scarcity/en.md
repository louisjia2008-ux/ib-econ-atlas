---
title: Scarcity
takeaway: Scarcity does not simply mean that something is rare; it means finite resources cannot satisfy every want at the same time, so every economy must choose.
definition: Scarcity is the condition in which the resources available to produce goods and services are limited relative to the needs and wants people would like to satisfy.
causalChain:
  - People seek more goods, services and opportunities
  - Land, labour, capital, time and entrepreneurship are constrained
  - Not every objective can be achieved simultaneously
  - Households, firms and governments must choose among feasible alternatives
  - Each choice gives up a next-best alternative and creates an opportunity cost
realWorldExample:
  title: Allocating freshwater between competing uses
  sourceUrl: https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal6
  observedAt: "2026-09-29"
  explanation: Safe drinking water, farming, industry and ecosystems all depend on freshwater. Even where water exists, the usable supply at a particular time and place is limited, so societies must set priorities among competing uses.
misconceptions:
  - Scarcity is not the same as an absolute shortage; it exists whenever resources cannot satisfy every possible use, even if shops have stock.
  - Money is not the only scarce resource; time, skills, natural resources and productive capacity are constrained too.
  - Poverty can intensify resource constraints, but affluent societies also face scarcity because resources still do not match every want.
examApplication:
  - An explanation of scarcity should connect limited resources, relatively unlimited wants and the resulting need to choose.
  - When evaluating a policy, ask which scarce resources it uses and which alternative uses are therefore given up.
summary:
  - Scarcity is a relative relationship between resources and wants.
  - Scarcity forces every economic agent to choose.
  - Opportunity cost expresses what a choice sacrifices.
---
Scarcity is the starting point of the economic problem. It is not a temporary empty shelf. It is the continuing constraint that available resources, time and productive capacity cannot accomplish everything people would like to do.

Economic questions therefore compare alternative uses. Two hours spent revising economics cannot also be spent in full on another subject. Land used for a hospital cannot simultaneously provide a park on the same site. Economics studies how such choices are made, who makes them and how their consequences are distributed.

To identify scarcity in a situation, ask whether a resource has competing uses and whether at least one feasible option must be forgone. If both answers are yes, the situation contains scarcity in the economic sense.
