---
title: Scarcity, choice, and cost on a PPC
takeaway: Points beyond a PPC reveal current scarcity, alternative points on the
  line represent choice, and movement along it reveals the other output forgone
  as opportunity cost.
definition: Within the PPC model, the frontier represents scarcity, alternative
  feasible combinations represent choice, and the reduction in one output when
  moving along the curve measures the opportunity cost of more of the other.
causalChain:
  - Resource constraints create a production boundary
  - Society selects one combination on the boundary
  - More horizontal-axis output requires reallocation
  - The lost vertical-axis output is its opportunity cost
realWorldExample:
  title: Fiscal resources trade off across public objectives
  sourceUrl: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/FM
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The IMF Fiscal Monitor examines limited fiscal space. Where budgets
    and administrative capacity are constrained, expanding one public service
    may require less of another, a logic represented by movement along a PPC.
misconceptions:
  - A point outside the curve is not impossible forever; it is unattainable with
    current resources and technology.
  - Opportunity cost is measured by forgone output, not by the geometric
    distance between two points.
examApplication:
  - Mark the starting point, destination, and quantity changes in both
    directions.
  - Link frontier position separately to scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost
    in the written explanation.
summary:
  - The frontier displays a current productive constraint.
  - Multiple combinations on the line require choice.
  - Movement along the frontier reveals the cost in the other output.
---
The PPC turns abstract scarcity into a visible boundary. A combination beyond the curve exceeds current capacity and cannot be produced now. Every point on the curve is feasible and efficient, but society must select one of them.

More of one output along the curve requires resources to leave production of the other. The fall in that second output is the opportunity cost of the increase. A complete answer states both quantity directions and their economic meaning.
