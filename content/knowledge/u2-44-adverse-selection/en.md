Buyers cannot distinguish quality or risk.  pooled prices drive good types away.  average quality falls and the market may shrink.

### Original scenario and reasoning

Illustrative example: used-car buyers cannot identify good cars and offer an average price. Good-car owners exit, worsening remaining quality. Inspection, warranties and credible certification can narrow the information gap, but certifiers must be trustworthy.

Adverse selection occurs before exchange, not as a post-contract behavioural change.

State whether the information problem occurs before or after exchange and evaluate credibility of remedies.
