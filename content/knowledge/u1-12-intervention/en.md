---
title: Intervention
takeaway: Intervention changes rules, prices, or resource allocation to
  influence behaviour; its benefits must be assessed alongside side effects and
  implementation costs.
definition: Economic intervention is deliberate action by a government, central
  bank, or other institution to alter market incentives, trading conditions,
  resource allocation, or income distribution.
causalChain:
  - Identify a gap between a market outcome and a social objective
  - Select a tax, regulation, spending, information, or other instrument
  - The instrument changes incentives and constraints
  - Behavioural responses produce intended and unintended outcomes
realWorldExample:
  title: London's congestion charge changes driving incentives
  sourceUrl: https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/driving/congestion-charge
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: Transport for London charges for driving in a defined area and
    period. The policy turns part of congestion's social cost into a monetary
    cost faced by drivers and aims to change routes, modes, or travel times.
misconceptions:
  - Announcing a policy does not achieve its objective; behaviour and
    implementation determine the result.
  - Intervention can correct a market problem while also creating government
    failure.
examApplication:
  - "Build the full chain: objective, instrument, incentive, behavioural
    response, and outcome."
  - Evaluation should consider elasticity, time lags, administrative cost, and
    affected stakeholders.
summary:
  - Intervention combines an objective with an instrument.
  - Policy works by changing incentives and behaviour.
  - Both effectiveness and side effects require evidence.
---
Market outcomes may conflict with objectives for efficiency, equity, stability, or sustainability. Institutions then use taxes, subsidies, regulation, public provision, or information to change economic conditions.

A policy does not turn an objective directly into an outcome. It first changes a price or constraint and agents respond. Unexpected behaviour, weak implementation, or poor information can reduce the net benefit or create a new problem.
