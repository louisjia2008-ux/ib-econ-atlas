---
title: Equity
takeaway: Equity judges whether a process or outcome is fair; it depends on an
  explicit value standard rather than on market quantities or averages alone.
definition: Equity is a normative concept concerning the fairness of the
  distribution of economic resources, opportunities, burdens, and outcomes,
  assessed through principles such as need, contribution, opportunity, or
  rights.
causalChain:
  - Identify how resources, opportunities, or burdens are distributed
  - Choose a principle of fairness
  - Compare the treatment and outcomes of different groups
  - Design or evaluate a policy that changes the distribution
realWorldExample:
  title: Comparing inequality and poverty policies
  sourceUrl: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/inequality-and-poverty.html
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: OECD work on inequality, poverty, and mobility shows how taxes and
    transfers alter disposable income across groups. It also demonstrates why an
    equity judgement must reveal the standard being applied.
misconceptions:
  - Equity need not mean that every person receives an identical amount.
  - A claim that a policy is fairer is incomplete unless it specifies for whom
    and according to which principle.
examApplication:
  - Name both the fairness criterion and the affected groups in an evaluation
    paragraph.
  - Do not assume an efficiency-equity trade-off; explain the mechanism that
    creates or avoids one.
summary:
  - Equity is a normative judgement.
  - Need, opportunity, and contribution are possible standards.
  - Equity analysis identifies groups and the distribution mechanism.
---
Economic policy changes not only total output but also who receives income, services, and opportunities. Equity analysis concentrates on that distribution and requires a stated principle for judging it.

Support according to need, reward according to contribution, and equal access to opportunity are all plausible ideas of fairness. Because they can point toward different policies, a strong argument makes its value standard visible rather than treating equity as a self-explanatory label.
