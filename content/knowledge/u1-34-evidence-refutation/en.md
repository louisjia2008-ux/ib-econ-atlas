---
title: Empirical evidence and refutation
takeaway: Empirical work compares theoretical predictions with observation;
  contrary evidence weakens or forces revision of a model, although one study
  rarely ends a complex debate.
definition: Empirical evidence comes from systematic observation, data, or
  experiment. Refutation occurs when credible evidence conflicts with a testable
  theoretical prediction, requiring that prediction to be rejected or revised.
causalChain:
  - A theory generates a clear observable prediction
  - Research design defines data and comparison
  - Evidence indicates whether the prediction is supported
  - Conflict prompts review of measurement, assumptions, or mechanism
realWorldExample:
  title: Randomised evaluation constructs a policy counterfactual
  sourceUrl: https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluations
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: J-PAL evaluations show how random assignment can create a
    comparison for what would have happened without a programme. Differences
    between treatment and control groups test a causal prediction in that
    setting.
misconceptions:
  - Failing to reject a hypothesis does not prove it is permanently true.
  - When evidence conflicts, data quality and research design need checking
    alongside the theory.
examApplication:
  - Assess a sample, measure, counterfactual, and external validity when judging
    evidence.
  - Use calibrated language such as supports, fails to support, or weakens
    rather than absolute proof.
summary:
  - Theory must yield testable predictions.
  - Research design limits what evidence can answer.
  - Refutation drives revision of assumptions and models.
---
An economic theory connects to the observable world only if it generates predictions that evidence could contradict. Statistical data, natural experiments, randomised trials, and historical comparison all help construct a credible counterfactual.

Evidence remains uncertain. Measurement error, sample differences, and context affect results. Convergence across replications and methods raises confidence, while contrary findings may narrow the domain of a claim or motivate a change in the mechanism.
