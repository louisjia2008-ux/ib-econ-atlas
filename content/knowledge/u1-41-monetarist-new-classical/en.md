Late twentieth-century monetarists criticised excessive confidence in fiscal fine-tuning and stable multipliers, drawing attention to policy lags and monetary stability. New classical models developed the role of forward-looking expectations.

These ideas shaped debates over independent central banks, inflation targets, and credible rules. Actual policymaking often combines traditions: it accepts short-run demand fluctuations while taking inflation expectations, supply constraints, and long-run sustainability seriously.
