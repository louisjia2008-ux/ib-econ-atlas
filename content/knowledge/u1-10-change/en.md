---
title: Change
takeaway: Economic outcomes evolve with technology, institutions, preferences,
  and shocks, so a model's conclusion must be located in time and under stated
  conditions.
definition: Change refers to adjustment over time in agents, resources,
  technology, institutions, or the external environment and to the resulting
  shifts in production, distribution, and well-being.
causalChain:
  - Technology, policy, or preferences change
  - Relative costs and incentives move
  - Households and firms adjust behaviour
  - Market structure, output, and distribution reach new outcomes
realWorldExample:
  title: A changing energy system
  sourceUrl: https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2025
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The IEA World Energy Outlook compares scenarios for energy
    technology, investment, and policy. It shows how costs, infrastructure, and
    policy gradually alter the energy mix and associated employment.
misconceptions:
  - Adjustment is rarely instantaneous; contracts, skills, and infrastructure
    create lags.
  - A before-and-after difference does not by itself prove that one policy
    caused the change.
examApplication:
  - Describe a dynamic process with a clear initial state, transmission chain,
    and later state.
  - Evaluation can address speed, reversibility, expectations, and short-run
    versus long-run adjustment.
summary:
  - The economic environment is not static.
  - Changing incentives work through behavioural adjustment.
  - The time path and causal identification both matter.
---
Technology, demography, preferences, policy, and external shocks alter economic constraints. Agents do not remain mechanically attached to previous choices; they learn, form expectations, and respond.

Change has both a speed and a path. Capital equipment, worker skills, or contracts can be fixed in the short run, while the long run permits wider adaptation. A dynamic answer should separate the trigger, the transmission process, and the later outcome rather than simply comparing two dates.
