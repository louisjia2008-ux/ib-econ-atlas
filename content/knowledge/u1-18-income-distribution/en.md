---
title: Income and output distribution
takeaway: Income distribution shapes each group's ability to obtain output;
  redistribution changes the market outcome through taxes, transfers, and public
  services.
definition: Income and output distribution describes how national income and
  goods or services are divided among people, households, or factors of
  production. Redistribution is a deliberate policy-induced change to the
  initial distribution.
causalChain:
  - Factor ownership and market returns generate original income
  - Income determines most market purchasing power
  - Taxes, cash transfers, and public services intervene
  - Disposable income and effective access to output are redistributed
realWorldExample:
  title: Taxes and transfers change disposable income
  sourceUrl: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/inequality-and-poverty.html
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: OECD inequality and poverty material compares income before and
    after taxes and transfers, showing how the same production outcome can yield
    different household distributions under different fiscal systems.
misconceptions:
  - Growth in total output does not guarantee a more equal distribution or a
    rise in every person's real income.
  - Redistribution is not confined to cash; free or subsidised public services
    alter effective access too.
examApplication:
  - Distinguish original income, disposable income, and public services received
    in kind.
  - Evaluate redistribution through equity objectives, incentives,
    administration, and targeting.
summary:
  - Factor returns shape original income.
  - Purchasing power affects access to market output.
  - Taxation, transfers, and services can redistribute.
---
Market income arises as wages, rent, interest, and profit and reflects factor ownership, skills, and bargaining power. Income then determines how strongly a person can express demand in markets, linking distribution directly to the question of for whom output is produced.

Progressive taxes, social protection, public education, and health provision can alter the result. Evaluation considers whether poverty or inequality falls alongside financing, work incentives, gaps in coverage, and service quality.
