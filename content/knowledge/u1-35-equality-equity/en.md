---
title: Equality and equity
takeaway: Equality describes identical or less dispersed distributions, while
  equity judges whether a distribution is fair; the two may overlap or equity
  may require different treatment.
definition: Equality is a condition of sameness or smaller differences in
  resources, opportunities, or outcomes. Equity evaluates whether treatment and
  distribution are justified by an explicit principle of fairness.
causalChain:
  - Observe differences in resources, opportunity, or outcomes
  - Select an equality measure and fairness principle
  - Ask whether identical treatment creates fair opportunity
  - Choose a universal or targeted policy response
realWorldExample:
  title: Inequality measurement differs from an equity judgement
  sourceUrl: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/inequality-and-poverty.html
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: OECD income-distribution evidence measures differences across
    groups. Whether a measured difference is fair still depends on value
    judgements about need, opportunity, contribution, and rights.
misconceptions:
  - Identical inputs may fail to create fair opportunity when starting positions
    differ.
  - Reducing one inequality does not secure equity in another dimension; incomes
    may converge while service access remains unequal.
examApplication:
  - Specify whether equality concerns income, wealth, opportunity, or outcome.
  - Use a clear fairness principle to justify equal or differentiated treatment.
summary:
  - Equality can be described and measured.
  - Equity requires a normative standard.
  - Equity may support differentiated treatment to address unequal starting
    points.
---
Equality concerns the degree of difference and may refer to income, wealth, educational opportunity, or health outcomes. Equity goes further by asking whether differences follow from a fair process and whether policy ought to change them.

Identical treatment can be equitable, as with equal pay for equal work. Targeted support can also be equitable, as when extra resources address a learning barrier. A sound judgement makes the comparison, starting position, and principle explicit.
