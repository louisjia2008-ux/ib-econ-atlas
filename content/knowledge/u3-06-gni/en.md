Domestic output forms GDP.  add residents' net primary income from abroad.  deduct capital consumption to obtain a net measure.

### Original scenario and reasoning

Illustrative example: GDP=500, primary income received from abroad=40 and paid abroad=25 give GNI=515. With depreciation 30, NNI=485. GDP versus GNI distinguishes domestic production from resident income, not different currencies.

Not every cross-border payment is primary income; distinguish primary income from transfers.

State indicators, formulas, units and limits; distinguish nominal, real, aggregate and per-capita measures.
