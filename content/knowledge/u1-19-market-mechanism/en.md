---
title: The market mechanism
takeaway: The market mechanism uses changing prices to coordinate decentralised
  supply and demand and to draw resources toward higher willingness to pay and
  expected profit.
definition: The market mechanism is the process through which supply and demand
  interact so that price signals, incentives, and rationing coordinate buying
  and selling decisions and allocate resources.
causalChain:
  - Desired purchases and sales do not initially match
  - Shortage or surplus puts pressure on price
  - Price changes consumer and producer incentives
  - Quantities and resources adjust toward a new market outcome
realWorldExample:
  title: Food prices communicate scarcity
  sourceUrl: https://www.fao.org/worldfoodsituation/foodpricesindex/en/
  observedAt: 2026-09-29
  explanation: The UN Food and Agriculture Organization Food Price Index
    summarises international food price movements. When a supply shock raises
    prices, the signal affects consumer substitution, stockholding, and future
    planting decisions.
misconceptions:
  - A market price reflects effective demand, not the same thing as social need.
  - Price can coordinate information without guaranteeing efficiency where
    market power, externalities, or asymmetric information exist.
examApplication:
  - Explain the signalling, incentive, and rationing functions of price.
  - Trace a supply or demand change through price and quantity to the movement
    of resources.
summary:
  - Price aggregates information about supply and demand.
  - Price changes incentives on both sides of a market.
  - Rationing by purchasing power need not be equitable or socially optimal.
---
No single participant in a market holds all relevant information. Price condenses the willingness to pay of many buyers and the costs of many sellers. A higher price encourages some producers to expand and causes some consumers to reduce demand or find substitutes.

That coordination is conditional. A market is more likely to perform well when prices reflect private and social costs and benefits, competition is meaningful, and information is available. Income inequality also means that measured demand partly reflects ability to pay.
